Games

In 2023, I began learning game development with Unity by following a series of tutorials. Before long, I entered my first game jam, where I put my developing skills to the test under time preasure.

My interest in learning new programming languages led me to discover the PICO-8 fantasy console, where I learned Lua. From there, I expanded into Python game development before eventually moving on to Godot.

The following slides are a collection of games I've developed over the last three years.

fish for chips

Fish for Chips is a game I made for the 2024 Black Cat Game Jam, which ran for one week with the theme “Chaos”. I collaborated with a musician to create the game's sound effects and music. Fish for Chips placed 36th overall out of more than 130 entries.

Fish for Chips follows a middle-aged cat who ventures into the sewers beneath a tavern to rescue his son, who has been kidnapped by a gang of rats. After prototyping a fun combat system, I spent most of the week developing the game's art, animations and story.

EyeBall-et HELL

EyeBall-et HELL is a game I developed in 2025 for a Year 11 VCE Media assessment. The task required us to create two related media products, so I chose to develop the game alongside a matching CD cover design.

By the end of the weekend, I was happy with the game's visual presentation, but I hadn't been able to fully implement the gameplay or story within the time limit. This taught me to avoid relying too heavily on narrative to carry a game's premise, particularly when its implementation is uncertain.

Crystal Liberators

Crystal Liberators is a game I developed over a weekend for the 220th Mini Jam, a fortnightly game jam. The theme was “Crystal” and the limitation was “Timing is Everything”.

Over the weekend I ended up with a product where I was happy with the way it looked but the gameplay and story couldn't be implimented within the time limit. I learned not to bank too much of the games premise on story especially when it's uncertain its executable.

FILMS

Since childhood, I have enjoyed creating stories through short animations, YouTube videos and comics. When I began my Year 10 Media course, I was eager to apply that creativity to filmmaking and begin exploring storytelling through film.

In Year 10, my partner and I created an interview-style documentary exploring the dangers of addiction, with a focus on video games. I continued making films with friends throughout the following year and into VCE, where I collaborated with Oli Vaughan on a seven-minute fight sequence featuring detailed choreography. Our final film together was submitted for Oli's VCE assessment and followed two brothers coping with the loss of their mother.

The following slides are a collection of films I directed or helped produce.

Blue Journey

Blue Journey is an 18-minute short film directed by Olivier Vaughan, following two brothers. I helped revise and plan the script and played the antagonist. Planning began in late March, with production wrapping up in September.

Working on Blue Journey taught me the time and effort required to produce a cohesive film longer than ten minutes.

Addiction documentary: gaming

This documentary was created in 2024 for a Year 10 Media assessment. The task required us to research a topic, interview people and edit the footage into a documentary featuring at least two interviewees and supporting B-roll.

Working on the documentary was my first experience with filmmaking and showed me the satisfaction of creating a finished product and sharing it with an audience.
